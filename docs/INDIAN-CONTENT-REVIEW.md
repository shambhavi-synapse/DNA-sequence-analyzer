# Molecular Notes: Indian research and editorial refinement

Review date: 7 November 2026. Branch: `review/seqscope-atlas`, draft PR #10, stacked on the unmerged scientific audit in PR #9. Main and GitHub Pages production are unchanged.

## What changed

The nucleosome hero now shares one dark editorial field with the heading, using a restrained edge mask and negative space. Tablet typography and artwork no longer overlap. The existing optimized illustrations remain intact. Replication and transcription use alternating, softly edged compositions; translation uses a quieter inset field. None is enlarged beyond the previous process-art limits. Genetics in Everyday Life follows the introduction. The later collection is titled **Genomic structure, variation and regulation**, because it includes genome organization, gene regulation and RNA processing as well as variation.

Research examples are separate from foundational explanations and distinguish studies in particular organisms, cohorts and assays. Soft shadows and selected states apply to interactive elements, with theme-specific tokens and reduced-motion guards. About uses Shambhavi Tiwari in the introduction and signature; the footer retains Shambhavi.

Browser testing also exposed nested ORF marks occupying the same coordinates on a frame track. Display-only subtracks now separate overlapping candidates, retaining candidate order, coordinates, proteins and the 250-candidate display limit. Tall maps scroll rather than compressing every track. The calculation engine is unchanged. ORF list surfaces now consistently use theme tokens; chart labels and interpretation text are more readable.

## Source balance and method

There are **35 unique external scientific references** in the Molecular Notes view, including the example-provenance and shared-reading areas. **20 (57%) are Indian institutional teaching/data resources or papers with verified India-based research affiliations.** This includes **14 distinct research papers**, two NPTEL courses, one IISc laboratory research resource, two Indian data resources and one DBT programme update. Repeated links count once. Source origin is not inferred from the hosting domain or a researcher's surname. International collaboration is identified explicitly.

The machine-readable catalogue in `INDIAN-SOURCES.json` records identifiers, publication details, affiliation evidence, source types and the verification date. Paper metadata was cross-checked against NCBI's PubMed records, with full publisher/author manuscripts used where older records list only the first author's affiliation. A paper hosted on PubMed can still report work conducted in India. Website links use DOI/publisher destinations where practical.

Indian teaching resources support foundational concepts. They are not presented as evidence that Indian researchers originally discovered the helix, base pairing, replication or the central dogma.

## Reference corrections and remaining gaps

Two pre-existing links were demonstrably unrelated to their adjacent topics: PMC4528411 concerns perceptual decisions, and PMC5774632 concerns transient CDK4/6 inhibition. They were replaced with Feschotte and Gilbert's endogenous-virus review and Rhodes and Lipps' G-quadruplex review. The telomere teaching link now points to Wellcome Connecting Science, with its actual resource named.

No sufficiently direct Indian research substitute was verified for the specific cephalopod neural RNA-editing example, the endogenous-retrovirus overview, caffeine metabolism, the original coriander GWAS or organism-specific genetic-code reassignment. Strong international sources remain. The Indian chemosensory study is clearly related research, not a replication of the coriander GWAS. The existing eye-colour foundation remains international, with Indian collaborative skin-pigmentation research kept distinct. No unsupported attribution was added to fill these gaps.

Some publishers restrict full text, and NCBI intermittently returned browser-check pages during retrieval. Titles, DOIs, authors and abstracts were independently retrieved through the authoritative PubMed metadata service. This is bibliographic and relevance verification, not a guarantee that every reader can access every publisher's full text without restriction. The course pages load; their dynamically generated syllabus link was defective during retrieval, so the verified IISc syllabus URL is recorded separately in the catalogue.
